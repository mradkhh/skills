# Storyboard

Write it before the full build. It turns a short idea into a plan you can execute, scene by scene and cut by cut.

## 1. Pull the brief apart

List: goal, audience, key message, required text (word for word), format, length, sound, allowed resources, outputs. Do not invent real facts about a product. Make every reversible creative decision yourself.

## 2. Find the through-line

One thread runs through the whole video: **what acts, what changes, and what the result proves.** It can be a material that transforms, motion handed from object to object, a chain of causes, a space that unfolds — or something new. Do not reuse the sequence of an earlier video under a new title.

A circle, a letter O, or a portal is one possible device, not a habit. Two or three devices may combine when they form one action.

## 3. Options per scene

For each scene, sketch **3–5 short options** that differ in action, composition and space, metaphor, or transition. A new colour or font alone is not a new option. Pick the strongest for the whole video and show the user the pick with a one-line practical reason. Do not show your internal reasoning.

## 4. The plan card

Fill one card per scene:

| Field | What to fix |
| --- | --- |
| Time and meaning | Interval, main accent, role in the story |
| States and action | Before, the trigger, what the hero does, the reaction, the result |
| Space | Composition, fore / mid / background, light, material |
| Text | Exact words, how the letters or words move, reading window |
| Camera and optics | Path, target, rotation and speed, shot size, focus and blur |
| Links | Entry from the previous shot, what carries the cut, exit to the next |
| Sound | Musical moment, SFX and the event they sync to — or explicit silence |
| Check | Main risk, how you will check it, a technical fallback |

The storyboard is a working plan, not a queue of approvals. When the user asked to approve steps, respect that.

## 5. Design cuts with both scenes

- **Match cut:** align the projected silhouette, scale, orientation, and direction of motion.
- **Mask transition:** the next scene must really appear inside the mask. An overlay after a hard cut is not a mask transition.
- **One location:** keep poses, perspective, light, and speeds continuous. Tie each reveal to a real overlap or action.
- **Meaningful cuts** between different places do not need one continuous world coordinate.

## 6. Probe

Before the full build, test the one riskiest moment at real quality, in motion, with sound when there is sound. Pick its length by what it must prove — do not force every event into a fixed 5–7 seconds. A probe does not replace the storyboard or the review after the full build.
