# Reference videos

## Work from real frames

- **File given:** work with its real frames and its audio.
- **URL given:** open the page and get what is really available first. Never describe editing, motion, frames, or music from a title or a link. The page may give only text or a poster — say how much you could access.
- **Not available:** name what you could not get. You can still direct an original piece, but do not credit it with observations you never made.

## Map the reference

Make a scene and cut map with timecodes: boundaries, lengths and shot sizes, composition and layers, the hero's action, typography, camera path and speed, material and light, what carries each cut, sound and rhythm.

For important cuts, extract a dense frame strip before, during, and after, and watch them at speed. A single still cannot tell exact speed, easing, or sound quality. Three chosen frames are not a frame-by-frame analysis.

Keep observation and transfer apart:

> **seen or heard at a given interval → the useful principle → your own solution for this story**

Keep the source and its access status. Studying a technique gives no right to put someone else's video, model, font, or music into the output.

## Combining several references

Use each one with intent. Write down which quality you take from each — for example, spatial composition from the first, typography from the second, camera and rhythm from the third (an example split, not a rule). Note what fits together and what conflicts, and build one shared system, not three separate fragments.

Adapt palette, logo, materials, hero, and setting to the request; they do not have to copy the sources. Keep one causal thread and one scale of motion. Show a short map — **reference → quality → how it is used** — then write your own storyboard.
